# The Prāṇic Load Unit (PLU): A Unified Human Performance Equation

This document defines the mathematical foundation of the Prāṇic Load Unit (PLU), a cross-disciplinary metric that translates phonetic sequences directly into objective physiological and cardiorespiratory strain values.

## 1. The Operational Four-Factor Equation
The calculation engine scores individual textual segments by feeding phonetic variables into a deterministic formula derived from high-performance exercise physiology models:

$$\text{Prāṇic Load (PLU)} = \text{Breath Duration (s)} \times \text{Vocal Intensity (\% MVE)} \times \text{Compound Density (CPS)} \times \text{Repetitions}$$

*   **Breath Duration (s):** The continuous exhalation timeframe required to articulate a passage between consecutive daṇḍa breath marks.
*   **Vocal Intensity (\% MVE):** Measured as a percentage of Maximum Voluntary Effort (MVE). Traditional lineage high-tone vocalization requires a sustained 85% MVE, placing high physical strain on the core abdominal and intercostal muscle walls.
*   **Compound Density (CPS):** Measures complex Sanskrit *saṃyukta* and *bīja* compounds executed per second, tracking structural glottal friction demands.

## 2. Sports Science Equivalency Model
By applying this metric, the model demonstrates that chanting *Namakam Anuvāka 8* under strict traditional constraints yields a single-phrase workload profile of **55.5 PLU**. In exercise physiology and physical therapy models, this is the sensory and metabolic equivalent of executing an **11-repetition set of Deadlifts at 80% of an athlete's 1-Repetition Maximum (1RM)**, initiating an intense, acute central nervous system (CNS) fatigue rather than simple physical tiredness.
