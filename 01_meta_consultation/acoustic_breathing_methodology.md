# Acoustic Breathing Capture Manual: Non-Invasive Field Testing Methodology

This document outlines the operational field methodology for tracking a chanter's exact respiratory cycles using high-fidelity close-range microphone acoustics instead of invasive respiratory bands, preserving an authentic performance envelope.

## 1. Acoustic Signature Waveform Tracking
By recording via an uncompressed 48kHz WAV audio interface with a cardioid microphone positioned 30 cm from the mouth, individual breath parameters leave distinct, measurable acoustic signatures that can be mapped inside software like Audacity:

*   **The Inhalation Signature (The Sharp Gasp):** Sudden, forced inspirations at double-daṇḍa (`॥`) marks register as distinct, high-amplitude, sharp audio gasps. These appear on a time-series plot as crisp, vertical transient spikes immediately preceding vocalization.
*   **The Exhalation Signature (The Visarga Hiss):** Forcing a complete lung clearance on words ending in a *visarga* (`namaḥ`) produces a clear unvoiced friction *hiss*. This registers as a predictable drop in background noise combined with sustained high-frequency acoustic tracking, verifying that the chanter is emptying their expiratory reserve volume.

## 2. Post-Hoc Verification Protocol
Following the recording session, the audio file is played back directly to the expert chanter. The researcher plots the time-series waveform on a screen, isolates the transient gasp spikes, and logs their timestamps. The chanter manually verifies whether each identified spike aligns with their subconscious, automatic inhalation points, establishing an empirical breath count with zero performance distraction.
